# Writing a small business site

Visitors skim. They want to know what's sold, whether it's for them, what it costs and what to do next. Every line on the page answers one of those, with this business's own facts.

## Get the facts first

`business_profile` read and `intake.md`: the services and prices, the town or area, opening hours, years in business, licences, guarantees, the team's names, real reviews. Ask the owner in one message for anything missing that the page needs. Never invent a review, a customer, an award, a licence number or a figure ("500+ happy customers"). On a demo site, made-up facts are fine but must be plausible and agree with each other on every page.

## The rules

- **Headlines say the thing.** Eight words or fewer. "Emergency plumbers in Leeds, there within the hour", not "Your Trusted Plumbing Partner". A heading names something: the service, the place, the promise.
- **The line under a headline** is one sentence of 25 words or fewer, with one hard fact (a price, a time, an area, a number of years).
- **Buttons say what happens**, three words or fewer: "Book a visit", "Get a free quote", "Call 555 0142", "See the menu". Never "Learn more", "Submit" or "Click here". One wording per action across the whole site (the design brief's button list).
- **Prices** wherever the business shares them, or "from" prices. A price is the most-read word on a service page.
- **Plain words.** Write the way the owner would say it to a customer at the door. Short sentences, active voice, "you" and "we".
- **No long dashes** (— or –). Use a full stop, a comma, a colon or brackets.
- **No stock marketing words or phrases**: elevate, seamless, unlock, unleash, empower, cutting-edge, world-class, state-of-the-art, bespoke (unless it's tailoring or joinery), synergy, leverage, robust, nestled, boasts, "look no further", "we've got you covered", "take it to the next level", "whether you're a...", "in today's fast-paced world", "it's not just a...". Replace each with the fact it stands in for.
- **No virtue words doing a fact's job**: "honest", "quality", "trusted", "passionate", "dedicated". Show it instead: "Fixed price before we start", "Licensed since 2009", "We tidy up and take the old boiler away".
- **Numbers as numerals**: "8 services", "30 years", "from $95".
- **Titles and labels in Title Case** (headings follow the kit's style).

## Page by page

- **Home:** what, where, the main action; then proof (licences, rating, years); services with "from" prices; how it works in three or four steps; reviews; one FAQ block; the main action again.
- **Service pages:** what the service is, signs you need it, what's included, the price or "from" price, how long it takes, two or three questions people ask, the main action.
- **About:** who runs it, since when, where, why customers choose them, the team with names and roles. One short story, true, not a mission statement.
- **FAQ:** the questions customers really ask (price, timing, area, guarantee, payment, what to expect), answered in two or three sentences each.
- **Contact:** phone, email, area or address (street and town; no house number on a demo), hours, the form, and what happens after they send it.
- **Thank-you pages:** what happens next and when ("We'll call you back within two working hours").
- **Page title and description** for search (Rank Math): the service and the town in the title, one plain sentence with the main action in the description.

## Check it

Run `anypress-slop-gate` on every page. Then read every visible string once more, out loud if you can: rewrite anything clumsy, unclear, repeated or trying to sound clever. Plain beats cute.
